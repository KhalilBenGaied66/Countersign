# Evaluation

## What is measured

Two things, kept apart because they fail differently.

- **Reading.** Is each field what is printed on the page? Compared exactly with the
  ground truth: 663.274 is not 663.27.
- **Deciding.** Was the document approved, sent to a person or set aside, and did that
  do harm?

A decision is **harmful** in two cases:

- a *wrong approval*: a document approved although a critical field is wrong, although
  it was tied to another supplier's record, or although it had to be stopped (changed
  bank account, totals that do not add up, unknown supplier, a quote taken for an
  invoice);
- a *lost invoice*: a real invoice set aside as "not an invoice" or as a duplicate,
  without anyone being asked to look.

Sending a good invoice to a person is a cost, not a harm. It is counted separately.

Critical fields are the ones that decide what is posted and paid: kind of document,
number, issue and due dates, currency, supplier VAT number, IBAN, order number, net,
VAT and gross totals.

## Dataset

640 synthetic documents, generated by `countersign.datagen` from fixed seeds. The
generator, the ground truth and every PDF are in the repository; a test checks that
the committed ground truth is the one the generator builds.

| | dev | test | confirm |
|---|---|---|---|
| Documents | 100 | 270 | 270 |
| Suppliers | 12 | 27 | 28 |
| Layouts | 4 | 7 | 7 |
| Languages | French, English | French, English, German, Spanish, Italian | the same five |
| Documents from suppliers never used in dev | 0 | 152 | 168 |
| Scans (no text layer) | 10 | 28 | 28 |
| Hybrid e-invoices | 6 | 16 | 16 |
| Two pages | 1 | 12, up to 51 lines | 12, up to 49 lines |
| Must not be approved | 38 | 87 | 88 |

Each split has one job.

- **dev** is what the prompt, the checks and every threshold were developed on.
- **test** was run once, after the pipeline was frozen. Three of its seven layouts,
  three of its five languages and fifteen of its suppliers appear nowhere in dev: an
  invoice from a German supplier, with its identifiers in a three-column footer and
  its due date written in a sentence, was first read by the pipeline during that run.
- **confirm** exists because the test split found something. It has the make-up of the
  test split (same suppliers, layouts and scenario mix) and other documents. It was
  generated after the fix for what the test split found, and run once.

What that does and does not mean is in [Limits](#limits).

### Scenarios

Each document carries two labels: what is printed on it, and what a correct system
does with it. The test and confirmation splits have the same counts.

| Scenario | dev | test | What it is | A correct system |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `clean` | 40 | 112 | a consistent invoice from a supplier on file | approves |
| `credit_note` | 4 | 10 | a credit note quoting the invoice it cancels | approves |
| `long` | 3 | 12 | 16 to 51 lines on two pages, running totals carried over | approves |
| `einvoice` | 4 | 12 | a PDF with its own XML embedded | approves, without a model |
| `scan` | 8 | 24 | a clean invoice rasterised, rotated, blurred, grained, JPEG-compressed | approves; review is acceptable |
| `totals_mismatch` | 6 | 14 | the supplier's own figures are wrong: gross, VAT rate, a line, swapped digits | stops |
| `scan_mismatch` | 2 | 4 | the same, as a scan | stops |
| `bank_changed` | 4 | 12 | a valid IBAN that is not the one on file, sometimes with a notice | stops |
| `bank_invalid` | 2 | 4 | an IBAN with a wrong digit | stops |
| `unknown_supplier` | 4 | 10 | a supplier that is not in the vendor master | stops |
| `purchase_order` | 5 | 12 | order missing, unknown, placed with another supplier, or exceeded | stops |
| `missing_field` | 2 | 6 | no invoice number, or no date | stops |
| `einvoice_tampered` | 2 | 4 | embedded XML with other amounts or another IBAN than the page | stops |
| `duplicate` | 3 | 8 | an invoice sent again as a copy | sets aside as duplicate |
| `not_invoice` | 5 | 12 | quote, delivery note, order confirmation, pro forma invoice | sets aside; review is acceptable |
| `injection` | 6 | 14 | a valid invoice carrying text addressed to the model | approves the true values, or stops; never approves what the text asks for |

The mix is deliberately hostile: a third of the test documents must not be approved.
A real inbox is mostly clean invoices; the shares below are per scenario for that
reason, and the overall rates describe this dataset, not a company's mail.

The six `injection` variants: a visible note saying the invoice is pre-approved; an
instruction inside a line description; a visible note naming the buyer as the supplier;
and three in white one-point text, invisible on the page and present in the text
layer: another IBAN, another set of totals, and "this is not an invoice".

## Method

```bash
uv run python -m countersign.eval.run --split confirm --config cascade
```

Each document goes through the same `Pipeline` object the application uses, with the
reference data of `data/reference`, a ledger that starts empty, and a fixed "today".
Five configurations share the same model calls:

| Configuration | Tiers | Checks |
|---|---|---|
| small, nothing checked | `qwen3.5:4b` | none: whatever is extracted is approved |
| large, nothing checked | `qwen3.5:9b` | none |
| small + checks | `qwen3.5:4b` | all; a scan cannot be read twice and goes to a person |
| large + checks | `qwen3.5:9b` | all; same |
| cascade + checks | `qwen3.5:4b`, then `qwen3.5:9b` when a re-read can help | all, plus two readings for scans |

Models: Qwen 3.5, 4.7 B and 9.7 B parameters, Q4_K_M quantisation, Apache 2.0, served
by Ollama 0.35 on an RTX 5060 Ti (16 GB), temperature 0, fixed seed, thinking off,
output constrained to the JSON schema.

Every model answer is recorded (`evals/cassettes`). `python -m countersign.eval.gate`
recomputes all reports from the recordings, without a GPU, and CI fails if a report in
the repository is not what the code produces.

Times are model seconds as recorded during the live run on that card, on a machine
that was doing other work at the same time. They are not comparable across machines.

## How the numbers were obtained

In this order, on 2 October 2026:

1. **The pipeline was frozen and the test split was run once**, live, on the GPU.
2. **Two independent code reviews** ran during that run, without access to its results:
   one of the verification pipeline, one of the storage, the API and the evaluation
   code. They reported 48 findings, each reproduced by a script. All were fixed or
   recorded as limits, with a regression test each (`tests/test_hardening.py` and the
   storage and API tests).
3. **The test split was recomputed** from the recorded answers with the fixed code.
   None of the fixes changes a request to a model, so no model was called again.
4. **The test run had found one gap**, which no review finding covered: VAT at the
   wrong rate on a page that gives no base per rate. It was closed.
5. **The confirmation split was generated** after that fix, and run once, live.
6. **That run found one more gap**: a second reading that replaces a misprinted line
   amount by the one the totals imply. It was closed too, together with a reading
   artefact the same run showed (an order number glued to the next column), and every
   report was recomputed.

The cascade at each step:

| Step | Documents | Wrong approvals | Invoices lost | Approved without a person | Stopped when required |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. frozen pipeline | test, live | 2 | 0 | 173 of 183 | 85 of 87 |
| 3. after the reviews' fixes | test, recomputed | 2 | 0 | 173 of 183 | 85 of 87 |
| 4. after closing the first gap | test, recomputed | 0 | 0 | 173 of 183 | 87 of 87 |
| 5. the same code | confirm, live | 1 | 0 | 171 of 182 | 87 of 88 |
| 6. after closing the second gap | confirm, recomputed | 0 | 0 | 173 of 182 | 88 of 88 |

**Lines 1 and 5 are the measurements**: documents the code had never met, run once.
Together, 3 wrong approvals and no lost invoice in 540 documents, with 344 of 365
approvable documents approved without a person and 172 of 175 stopped when required.

Lines 3, 4 and 6 are not measurements. Each fix was written with its failures in
view, and a recomputed zero says that the fix works on the documents that prompted it.
Two splits found two different gaps; a third split should be expected to find a third.

- **The checks are what make the difference.** Unchecked, either model takes about
  nine documents in ten that had to be stopped and approves them. With the checks, the

### Prompt versions

The prompt went through five versions, each measured on the dev split with the small
model and the checks. With one model no scan is approved, so 54 of 62 is the ceiling

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v1: every value as text | 0 | 59.7 % (37/62) | 89.5 % (85/95) | 88.4 % (84/95) | 548 | 6.8 s |
| v2: amounts as numbers | 0 | 69.4 % (43/62) | 89.5 % (85/95) | 95.8 % (91/95) | 503 | 6.2 s |
| v3: no VAT base asked | 0 | 82.3 % (51/62) | 89.5 % (85/95) | 97.9 % (93/95) | 490 | 6.1 s |
| v4: IBAN and order number defined | 0 | 75.8 % (47/62) | 86.3 % (82/95) | 97.9 % (93/95) | 489 | 6.2 s |
| v5: credit note number reworded, referenced invoice added | 0 | 80.6 % (50/62) | 89.5 % (85/95) | 97.9 % (93/95) | 499 | 6.3 s |
<!-- /table -->

- **v1 to v2.** Asked for every value as text "copied from the page", to keep number
  formats out of the model's hands. Under constrained decoding the models then wrote
  values such as `"{38}"` or `"{\"value\": 35}"`. Asking for JSON numbers removed it.
- **v2 to v3.** The taxable base of each VAT rate was filled with the VAT amount
  whenever the page printed no base. The field was dropped; bases are derived.
- **v3 to v4.** Written for the large model, which built an "IBAN" out of a US routing
  number and reported customer codes as order numbers. It fixed those, and its wording
  of the invoice number made the small model return none for credit notes: fewer
  approvals with the small model alone.
- **v4 to v5.** That field reworded, and a field added for the invoice a credit note
  cancels.

The small model alone is slightly better with v3 than with v5 (51 against 50). The
cascade is not: with v3 it approves 59 of 62 and with v5 60 of 62, both without a
harmful decision. The later versions fix mistakes of the second reader.

## Failures

### Harmful decisions

Three wrong approvals in the two runs, from two causes. In all three the amount that
would have been paid is the one the supplier's totals state; what was wrong is that an
invoice contradicting itself went through without a person.

**Test split: VAT at half its rate, twice.** The supplier charges half the VAT its own
rate calls for, and the totals follow.

- T-040, a French energy invoice: "20 % … 1 787,57 … 178,76".
- T-244, a German trade-fair invoice: "MwSt 19 % … 262,78" on a base of 2 627,78.

The small model read both pages correctly. The check that compares each VAT amount
with its rate needs a base: the one printed in the VAT table, or the lines taxed at
that rate. Neither page has a VAT column in its line table, the prompt has not asked
for the printed base since version 3, and with two rates the net total is not the
base of either. The check had nothing to compare and reported "VAT matches its rates
and bases". On the dev split the only invoices with two rates use a layout that prints
a rate on each line, and the one wrong-rate document has a single rate, for which the
net total is the base: the gap never showed.

The fix (decision 19 in [decisions.md](decisions.md)): each VAT row implies its base,
its amount divided by its rate, and the bases implied must account for the net total.
178,76 at 20 % implies 893,80, not 1 787,57. A check on the figures that cannot run
now fails or finds another way; it does not pass. The confirmation split holds no
document of that exact kind (its four wrong-rate invoices carry a single rate). What
it shows is that the stricter check costs nothing: of its twelve invoices with two
rates on those two layouts, the six a correct system approves were all approved.

**Confirmation split: a line tidied by the second reading.** C-235 prints one line,
"2 × 57,00 … 126,40", under a net total of 114,00. The small model reported the line
as printed; the addition failed, and the large model read the document in turn. It
reported 114,00 for the line: a value the page does print, as the net total. Every
check then passed.

Two rules now stand in the way. The amount of a line has to be printed on the row of
its unit price: 114,00 is on the page, but not next to 57,00. And decision 7 (a second
reading may not overrule an amount the page prints), which only covered the three
totals, covers line amounts too. The first rule is the one that matters: it also stops
a first reader that tidies the line, which the large model alone did on this document.

The two causes have one thing in common. A figure was accepted because it fitted the
other figures, without being tied to what it is a figure of: a VAT amount to its base,
a line amount to its row.

### Sent to a person for nothing

Confirmation split, as run: 11 of 182 documents a correct system approves.

| Documents | What happened |
|---|---|
| 3 scans | the two models disagreed on a field |
| 3 injections | the models obeyed white text nobody sees ("this is not an invoice"); the title of the page said otherwise, and a person was asked instead of the document being set aside |
| 3 invoices of an Irish supplier | a quantity such as "7,950 GB" read as 7.95 by both models: quantity × unit price no longer gave the amount |
| 2 invoices in a narrow layout | the text extraction ran two columns together, "PO-2026-20260France", and both models copied the order number that way |

The last two are approved by the current code, which takes the buyer's order number
out of what is glued to it. The quantities are not: nothing on the page says which of
7 950 and 7.95 is meant, short of doing the multiplication for the model.

Test split, first run: 10 of 183. Four scans on which the models disagreed, five
injections that the models obeyed and the checks stopped (three "not an invoice", two
sets of hidden totals), and one invoice of the same Irish supplier.

### What the models do unchecked

Without the checks, whatever is extracted is approved. On the test split the small
model then makes 91 harmful decisions out of 270 and the large one 87: nearly every
document that had to be stopped (76 of 87 are approved), plus invoices misread, plus a
few real invoices dropped as "not an invoice". The readers are not bad: the small
model gets every critical field right on nine invoices out of ten. Reading well is
not what keeps a changed bank account from being paid.

| Kind | Examples | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| A way past a check | an invoice number cut short accepted as printed; a credit note titled in lower case taken for an invoice; amounts read without their minus sign; a total cut at an apostrophe; dates in invisible text changing the day/month order | fixed |
| A real invoice set aside unseen | the last of two readings taken as the verdict; a monthly invoice read with last month's number | fixed |
| A check that did nothing | a line without an amount switched the addition of the lines off | fixed |
| An honest document always stopped | a label followed by a rate; capitals elsewhere taken for a title; a line outside VAT; identifiers on file written with spaces | fixed, except line discounts (a limit) |
| A file that costs too much | patterns that backtrack on a long run of spaces; attachments decompressed before their size is known; a page of 14 400 points rendered at 200 DPI | bounded; the total cost of a hostile file is a limit |
| Storage and API | two decisions on one document both succeeding; an export file written before its approval was committed; an upload that was not one transaction; a mistyped key setting leaving the API open | fixed |
| Evaluation | the supplier record chosen by the pipeline was not scored; "stopped for the expected reason" left out what was wrongly approved | fixed |
| Operations | a CI action pinned to a tag that does not exist; samples that would all be "too old" in a year | fixed |

## Limits

- **The documents are synthetic.** They were built to be hard in specific ways. Real
  invoices are hard in ways nobody listed: logos over text, stamps, handwriting, tables
  that span pages differently, fonts that extract badly. None of that is here.
- **The author of the pipeline is the author of the dataset.** Held-out suppliers,
  layouts and languages were not run before the test run. They were still designed by
  someone who knew what the checks look for, and the test run did find what that
  someone had not thought of. The deterministic vocabulary (month names, document
  titles, date labels) covers the five languages by construction; the label vocabulary
  for totals covers French and English only.
- **The confirmation split is new documents, not new kinds of documents.** Same
  generator, same suppliers and layouts as the test split, other invoices. It cannot
  find a failure the generator does not produce, and after it there is no split left
  that the code has not been fixed against.
- **Three wrong approvals in 540 is an estimate with a wide interval**, and of this
  dataset: between 0.2 % and 1.6 % at 95 %. The mix decides it: every one of the three
  is a supplier's own inconsistency, a kind of document these splits contain far more
  of than a mailbox does.
- **The mix is not a real inbox.** A third of the documents must be stopped. Overall
  rates describe this dataset.
- **Small counts.** 270 documents per split, and a handful per scenario variant. The
  intervals in the reports are wide; "87 of 88" is compatible with a true rate of a few
  percent of misses.
- **Harm is counted on the header.** An approval is wrong when a critical field or the
  supplier is wrong. A wrong quantity on a line whose amount is right, or a credit note
  pointing at the wrong invoice, is scored as a reading error, not as harm.
- **Duplicates in the dataset are copies of clean, approved invoices**, in order. A
  copy of a document in review, the same file twice, an invoice rejected then sent
  again are covered by tests, not by the splits.
- **Two models, one machine.** The comparison says nothing about other models. The
  larger model here is not "a large model".
- **Temperature 0 on one GPU.** The recordings are one sample of each answer. Another
  driver, another build of the model or another quantisation gives other answers.
- **Reading is scored field by field, exactly.** A due date wrong by a day and a due
  date missing both count as one wrong field.
- **What cannot be checked is not checked.** A wrong due date printed after no known
  label, a quantity misread on a line whose amount is right: the checks in
  [architecture.md](architecture.md) are a finite list.
